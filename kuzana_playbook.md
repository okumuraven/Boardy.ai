  founder/business in the program. Deliberately *not* "investee," "subsidiary," "startup," or

## 3. Mission, strategy, and how Kyle spends his time

**Mission**: "Kuzana: to help each other grow." Goal: **mint 1,000 more millionaires by 2040**.
Thesis (their own words): *"Kuzana makes SMEs investable by enforcing key operating disciplines
early, where traditional investors do not, and by creating liquidity at the fund level rather than
through individual exits."*

- **Diagnosis**: Kenyan SMEs are the real growth engine but are "uninvestable" — illiquid, poor
  governance/accounting. Existing investors chase sexy sectors (climate/gender/tech), use
  Silicon-Valley-sized ticket sizes that don't fit SMEs, and won't roll up their sleeves on basic
  business discipline.
- **Guiding policy**: SMEs, with a little "elbow grease" (operating discipline), are attractive
  investment targets.
- **Actions**: raise capital → find good companies (~1% acceptance) → build them into excellent
  companies → exit via **publicly listing Kuzana itself**, not selling underlying investments after
  ~7 years (a fund-level liquidity model, not a per-company exit model).
- **Focus philosophy**: explicitly benchmarks against Y Combinator (Airbnb/Stripe/Coinbase/
  DoorDash/Twitch/Reddit/Dropbox) vs. 500 Startups vs. Techstars — the argument that in accelerators,
  returns concentrate almost entirely with whoever is *the best*, not merely good, so "the only
  competition is ourselves."
- **Kyle's own JD & KPIs** (Chief Executive Officer & Founder): 3 rotating monthly priorities —
  raise money / find great companies / build excellent companies. Owns vision & strategy, team &
  culture, founder programs & portfolio (**makes all investment decisions**), partnerships &
  ecosystem, operations & finance, investor relations. KPI cadence: daily (clear messages in 24h,
  unblock founders, post for leads), weekly (leadership meeting, review scorecards, advance one
  partner/pipeline conversation), monthly (hit portfolio targets, publish an insight, investor docs),
  quarterly (OKRs, full portfolio valuation, investor report + shareholder meeting, evaluate founders
  to advance/intensify/exit), yearly (progress toward the 2040 goal, annual strategic plan/budget,
  major partnerships, annual founder performance report).

## 4. The investment/deal-flow pipeline (separate from, but adjacent to, Connect)

This is Kuzana's core accelerator funnel — the funding-application side, distinct from Connect's
peer-networking problem, but directly relevant to the "investor/lender as deal-flow consumer" finding
in [[kuzana_connect_discovery]].

**Screening criteria**: targets companies that can scale to ~Ksh100m/month revenue profitably within
5 years, at a *reasonable* valuation (1-2x revenue at most) — explicitly **rules out "pedigree"
founders** (ex-Google, Harvard) commanding 10-30x revenue valuations. Must be revenue, not GMV.
Capital-hungry-but-scalable models (solar leasing, BNPL, motorcycle manufacturing) are treated as a
red flag even if they can scale.

**Sourcing applicants**: founder decks, funder referrals, scanning supermarket shelves for products,
Instagram, exporter lists, LinkedIn posts, agriculture/manufacturing associations. A detailed
cold-outreach script exists for **bank partnerships** (positioning Kuzana as a de-risking partner
that makes SMEs "bankable" so banks can safely lend to them) — full SPIN-style question script
(Situation/Problem/Implication/Needs-payoff) included in the source doc.

**Investment process** (stage → purpose → typical deadline from batch-open):
Screening App (get email, Wk1) → Full App (financials/relevance, Wk1) → Call (needs, personality,
computer literacy, timeliness, viability, Wk2) → Ask for Documents (mgmt accounts, CR12, gross
profit, bank-statement revenue verification, Wk2) → DD Visit (verify on-ground, match revenue to
bank/accounting records, 5 customer + 5 creditor references, Wk3) → Reference Check (3 creditors
incl. any overdue, credit reference check, Wk3) → Finance Check (tax compliance, profitability
post-compliance, Wk4) → Intro to 3+ Sector Experts (triangulate the key risk, Wk6) → Board Approval
(email confirmation) → Verbal Offer → Written Summary Offer → Full Offer w/ legal docs → Group Q&A
with Lawyer → Share Transfer → Accounting Fix (must hit 90% before funds release) → **1st tranche**
→ Program Starts → **2nd tranche** (conditional on milestones: scalable email server, pro accounting/
ERP/CRM, monthly mgmt accounts by the 5th, Strategy Board formed with stock options, sales/marketing
strategy, dataroom, tax compliance) → apply for follow-on funding.

No NDAs are typically signed (a template exists only if an applicant insists). Referral bonus program
exists at `kuzana.co/referral`.

**"Reasons a company should not work with Kuzana"** (Kyle's own candid disclosure, worth knowing
verbatim for tone): most sales done is $3M, most raised is $20M, hasn't run a business >$360k/year,
not Kenyan himself, will argue convincingly even against a founder's better instinct, hasn't built in
their specific industry, gives blunt "American-style" criticism, demands a level of focus that will
strain some personal relationships, and **only invests in businesses with demonstrated founder-driven
customer acquisition ("demand ownership")** — explicitly not just a repeatable process someone else
runs.

## 6. Programme delivery — workshops, Strategy Board, Bizi Buddy System

**Workshops** run on an active-learning model, explicitly *not* lecture-style: 10 min content → 2 min
peer sharing → 5 min front-of-room share + leader feedback → 20 min implement-in-your-business →
repeat. Guest/expert speakers are capped at **4 minutes at a time** (cites Minerva University's
model). Philosophy: "the goal is not for you to prove you're smart, it's to transfer relevant
knowledge" — workshop leaders are told a single hour-long presentation is "too many insights to be
useful." Feedback is bidirectional and fast: Bizi give workshop-leader feedback via a "criticism
sandwich," Kuzana gives Bizi weekly feedback (one thing going well + one improvement area, always
sandwiched, always ends encouraging). Late/absent Bizi are charged **$50/hour or $400/full-day**,
deducted from their Bizi Growth Budget.

**Strategy Board** (Growth Council) — the manual, human version of what Connect's matching engine
would eventually need to support at scale:
- Kyle personally chairs; matches each Bizi to a Strategy Advisor and onboards advisors.
- Recurring monthly meeting per company with a strict agenda: intros (5m) → accounts + Top KPI
  (5m) → previous month highlights/lowlights (5m) → review of previous action items (5m) → any
  co-founder area report (3m) → **20 minutes of open Q&A with the Strategy Advisor** → next-month
  plan (5m).
- Attendees: CEO, other founder, the Strategy Advisor, another Bizi member, and someone from Kuzana
  to take notes. CEOs are reminded to send accounts + their "key question for the board" 5 days
  ahead so the meeting stays discussion-focused, not status-reporting.
- A "Mentor Manifesto" is signed by advisors as part of onboarding.

**Bizi Buddy System** — new in this pass, and the closest existing precedent to Connect's
peer-matching mechanic. Each Bizi is deliberately paired with a buddy **from a different company**
in the same batch. Responsibilities:
- Weekly check-ins, honest feedback, acting as a trusted sounding board before major decisions.
- **Workshop/session accountability**: buddies keep each other on time and attending; if one misses
  a session, the other briefs them and shares materials (or both loop in the Kuzana team for a
  recap if neither can cover the gap).
- **Decision consultation**: before major professional decisions (strategy changes, escalating
  operational problems, role changes), a Bizi is expected to consult their "growth council,"
  including their buddy.
- **Early-warning/peer accountability**: buddies raise concerns to the Kuzana team if they observe
  real risk to their partner's business.
- **Confidentiality**: what's shared between buddies stays confidential unless there's risk to the
  org/team, escalation is needed, or the Bizi consents to sharing.
- Also acts as first-line conflict mediation between Kuzana and the buddy's company.

**Bizi Growth Budget / accounting cadence**: twice-weekly (Mon/Thu) accounting meetings with the CEO,
finance controller, a "Zoho implementer," and bookkeeper if relevant — Bizi is made to screen-share
and eventually run the meeting themselves (a deliberate "teach them to drive" progression: Kuzana
runs it once, then the founder runs it, then the founder delegates it to their own bookkeeper).
Kuzana gets view-only reporting access. First-batch lesson: **Bizi don't proactively take offered
support** — Kuzana has to actively push them to complete things like marketing videos.

**Working with the Bizi — coaching philosophy**: aim for 4 positives before any critical feedback;
"tell us the problems, the good things will take care of themselves"; drive-building tactics span
identity (podcast of a successful founder, tie daily actions back to a stated goal), agency
(whiteboard their plan, remove people holding them back, establish a workout routine — "more
effective and longer-lasting than anti-anxiety meds"), and mastery (tough love, deliberately give a
wrong answer to provoke pushback, invest money and let them rise to the occasion). Each company gets
exactly **"one thing"** to focus on at a time (e.g. "solve churn before we discuss anything else").
Anonymous end-of-program feedback is collected via a form gated at 7 responses before Kyle looks (one
per founder, decoupled numeric-scale from written comments so ratings can't be tied to a person).
Testimonials are solicited on Sunlight Reviews, LinkedIn recommendations for Kyle, and tagged
LinkedIn posts (with the application link seeded in the comments).

## 7. Community & WhatsApp management (full detail — supersedes the shorter version previously in
this file)

No dedicated WhatsApp business account — runs on personal/team numbers, cross-posted to other
socials. **Groups are set to "hidden"** — not discoverable by random joiners; an admin manually adds
people. A general community link on kuzana.co is the public funnel-in point.

**Group hierarchy** (a Bizi can be in 5+ of these simultaneously):
- All-Bizi (whole community)
- Per-batch (e.g. "Jan 2025")
- Per-company (e.g. "Bellfeeds + Kuzana")
- Per-company Strategy Board
- Per-company Accounting
- Per-company Marketing

**Weekly content cadence** (fuller than the "General/MM/WW/FF" shorthand used elsewhere in the doc):
Friday Failzz → Saturday gratitude → Sunday next-week focus → Monday **Market Monday** (post your
product) → Tuesday AMA → Wednesday **Wisdom Wednesday** (poll/meme/best social find) → Thursday
feedback.

**Onboarding/moderation loop**: ask new members to introduce themselves + their business → send a
welcome note after each intro, asking for anything missing → **eject members who aren't business
people or never introduce themselves** → moderate and redirect off-topic threads to the right
sub-group → pin guidelines and repost them after any influx of new members → post reminders with a
link on each themed day → explicitly prompt specific people for input if a thread goes quiet →
cross-pollinate WhatsApp content with other socials in both directions.

**Growth targets** (as of the source doc): WhatsApp community doubling to **400 members** (~200 at
the time), explicitly gated on "maintaining quality," not open growth. LinkedIn doubling to **2k
followers** by end of June, explicitly "position for investors" — LinkedIn is being run as an
investor-facing channel as much as a founder one. A **paid/"premium" WhatsApp sub-group** was an
active idea under discussion, not yet built.

**Moderation-vs-discovery tension** (from [[kuzana_connect_discovery]], cross-referenced here): two
members independently said the community *feels* too restrictive, which paradoxically makes organic
discovery harder — worth weighing against the deliberate "eject non-introduced members" policy above,
since aggressive gatekeeping and easy discovery are somewhat in tension by design.

**Ownership gap**: per the CG Handover table (§1), day-to-day WhatsApp moderation ownership is
literally marked "?" as of the last handover — recruiting/briefing moderators and daily quality
checks have no confirmed owner.

## 8. Marketing / growth funnel and the CRM/tooling stack

**Core philosophy**: not optimizing for vanity metrics — the goal is to "attract ambitious founders,
build trust and authority, create founder aspiration, and convert high-potential entrepreneurs into
applications, meetings, and investments." Explicitly *not* trying to be a generic entrepreneurship
page — trying to be "the most respected founder ecosystem brand in Kenya." Content should make a
founder feel **"I want to become the kind of entrepreneur Kuzana backs"** (identity/aspiration
marketing, not information marketing).

**Funnel stages**: Discovery (Reels/TikTok/LinkedIn/founder clips, hook in first 3-5 seconds, e.g.
"We don't invest if…", "Most founders fail here") → Lead Capture (every source/campaign tracked via
UTM-style params, e.g. `form.kuzana.co/apply?source=instagram&campaign=april21vid`) → Application
Funnel (the real leverage point is *improving conversion of incomplete applications*, not driving
more raw traffic — priorities: follow-up speed, reminders, email deliverability, **WhatsApp
follow-up**, clear CTAs, CRM organization).

**Incomplete-application follow-up sequence**: reminder email → WhatsApp reminder → "need help?"
nudge → urgency/deadline reminder. Messaging rules: short, direct, action-oriented, avoid long
explanations, obvious CTA (e.g. "Complete your application," "Book your tea meeting").

**CRM & pipeline philosophy** — directly relevant to how Kuzana will evaluate whether Connect is
"overbuilt": *"The CRM exists to maintain follow-up consistency, reduce founder leakage, track
founder progress, and improve accountability. Not to create administrative overhead."* **Current
recommended stack, short-term: Streak CRM + Gmail + WhatsApp follow-up + Google Sheets dashboards.**
Explicit operating principle: **"avoid overcomplicated tooling too early — the bottleneck is
operational consistency, not software sophistication."** Recommended stack for the Bizi companies
themselves (not Kuzana internally): Zoho CRM or Bigin.

**Content production pipeline** (per-piece checklist, tracked in a marketing sheet with content codes
like `K000X`): 4-point framework (one person/one problem/one solution/one CTA) → hook/body/CTA shot
in multiple takes → CapCut edit (silence cut, noise reduction, logo, captions, cover) → per-platform
captions + UTM-tagged, Bitly-shortened links → sent to Kyle for approval → posted at scheduled time
→ performance logged after 48 hours. Posting cadence target: **2 posts/day across 4 platforms**,
prioritizing consistency over perfection. Content buckets: Founder Advice, Success Stories,
Events & CTAs. Reels = discovery/distribution; Posts/Carousels = authority/evergreen value.

**Top-level marketing KPIs tracked**: qualified leads, completed applications, incomplete→complete
conversion rate, tea meetings booked, response time, cost per lead, content engagement, application
quality, investment pipeline.

**Named operational risks** (their own list): founder follow-up inconsistency, weak CRM hygiene,
unclear ownership, poor attribution tracking, dependency on founder energy, operational bandwidth
limits. Their own diagnosis: *"the problem is usually not lack of content — it's weak packaging,
inconsistent systems, unclear positioning, weak follow-up, or operational leakage."*

**Lessons learned, stated in-doc** (what didn't work): overly polished pro-camera footage
underperformed casual phone video; too many flashy cuts distracted from the message; reposting via a
third-party scheduler (Metricool) hurt reach because platforms deprioritize non-native posts;
identical cross-platform reposts (no per-platform tailoring) underperformed; played it too safe
creatively.

## 9. Organizing Data — Kuzana's own data-modeling philosophy

A full internal "database rules" style-guide (15 rules) lives in the Organizing Data tab, framed as
"you don't need to learn SQL, you need to structure information so it can be filtered, summarized,
automated, and understood by someone other than the person who created it." Default tool: **Google
Sheets**, first resort. Notable rules directly relevant to how Connect's own data model should be
explained back to Kuzana, or how any CSV/export/reporting surface should be designed for them:
one table per sheet; one row = one real-world thing (never mix levels, e.g. companies and payments in
the same table); one column = one attribute (never combine two facts in a cell); use a `type`/category
column instead of near-duplicate tables (e.g. one Companies table with a Batch column, not
Batch1/Batch2/Batch3 tables); give every important record a stable ID (email or company name, not a
mutable display name); enter each fact exactly once (single source of truth, not repeated across
sheets); never encode meaning in cell color/bold/position — always a real column; standardize
categorical values (one canonical spelling per status, dropdowns wherever possible); store real dates/
numbers/currencies, not phrases like "last Friday" or "450k-ish"; **blank means unknown, zero means
zero** — never conflate them; never merge cells in a data table; keep raw data separate from
reports/dashboards; record repeating events as new rows, not new columns (e.g. a Payments table, not
a January/February/March column); every table needs one named owner; document ambiguous columns on a
definitions sheet. Ends with a "five-question test" before approving any new sheet.

This is a genuinely rigorous, computer-literate way of thinking about data for a team running
entirely on Google Sheets/Docs + Streak CRM — worth keeping in mind if Connect ever needs to export
data back to Kuzana or explain its own schema to them; they'll evaluate it against these exact rules.

## 10. Operating culture and philosophy (Kyle's personal playbook entries)

Mostly general leadership/productivity philosophy rather than Connect-specific, but useful for
understanding how Kyle thinks and communicates:

- **"Finding Answers" algorithm**: step 0 — is this actually a problem that needs solving now, by
  me? → 1. Try to figure it out yourself (5 min) → 2. Google/GPT (5 min) → 3. Ask a colleague or
  Kyle → 4. Test, learn, try again. Framed against a binary "ask for help vs. figure it out alone"
  false dichotomy — the point is efficient escalation, not self-reliance for its own sake.
- **Mistake tolerance**: explicitly budgets for **~10% of investments to fail** in year one (with a
  decaying failure curve in later years) — "if we aren't making mistakes we probably aren't taking
  enough risk." Warns against a system that keeps adding process to eliminate every mistake, since
  that eventually kills throughput entirely.
- **Privacy tiers** (four levels): Tier 1 fully open to everyone at Kuzana (playbooks, meeting notes,
  strategy docs, tooling, investment philosophy — "the value isn't the document, it's the people
  executing"); Tier 2 founder-private (revenue, cash, investor conversations, legal issues, cap
  tables); Tier 3 Kuzana-team-only evaluation layer (internal investment memos, founder assessments,
  "trust battery" scores, partner debates — explicitly to preserve honest internal discussion without
  it leaking back to founders); Tier 4 never shared (phone numbers, personal emails, family info,
  medical issues, addresses).
- **Feedback philosophy**: give it immediately, not saved for a formal review cycle — cites the Steve
  Jobs anti-trust deposition anecdote (no formal review file existed at all, feedback was always
  direct and immediate).
- **Building trust**: "the easiest way to build trust is to make a mistake and then go out of your
  way to fix it" (cites the 1990s Lexus recall).
- **Interruptions/focus**: turn off all notifications except calendar + WhatsApp-on-vibrate during
  scheduled deep-focus blocks; plan the next day the night before; a Sunday review of the past/coming
  week.
- **Negotiating**: "don't let them put the monkey on your back" — answer a vague complaint by asking
  the other party to be specific, rather than pre-emptively explaining/apologizing.
- Also present but purely personal/tangential to Connect: a "de-jank your setup" essay on ergonomics
  and workspace design, typing-speed advice, internet-connection troubleshooting (latency vs.
  bandwidth vs. instability, PingPlotter), Google Docs/file-naming/folder-structure conventions
  (files <39 characters, no version numbers in filenames — rely on File > Version History), and a
  clipboard-manager recommendation.

## 12. What this means for how Connect should be built (synthesis, carried over + expanded)

- **Strategy Board and the Bizi Buddy System are the two real, already-running precedents for
  matching** — one advisor↔founder (Kyle does it by hand monthly), one peer↔peer (formal buddy
  pairing with weekly accountability). The sharpest internal pitch for Connect may be "the tool that
  scales what Kyle and the Buddy System already do manually," not a general-purpose networking app.
  Worth revisiting whether Connect's role taxonomy should mirror these real internal categories
  (Bizi, Strategy Advisor, Buddy) rather than founder/developer/designer/investor.
- **Kuzana's own stated bias is against overcomplicated tooling** ("the bottleneck is operational
  consistency, not software sophistication") — a real objection to be ready for, not just assumed
  away. Their entire current stack is Streak CRM + Gmail + WhatsApp + Google Sheets; anything Connect
  ships should be easy to justify against that baseline, not just "more sophisticated."
- **WhatsApp is doing double duty** — it's both the community/networking layer (Connect's actual
  target) *and* a formal step in the paid-acquisition application-follow-up funnel (a Connect-
  adjacent but distinct concern). Don't conflate the two when scoping what Connect should absorb.
- **The Bizi Buddy System's confidentiality rules and buddy-pairing logic (cross-company, same-batch,
  weekly cadence, decision-consultation triggers) are a ready-made spec** if Connect ever builds a
  structured accountability-partner feature, distinct from its current AI-matched networking flow.
- **Data-model literacy**: Kuzana has a genuinely rigorous, self-authored data-hygiene standard
  (§9). If Connect ever needs to hand data back to Kuzana (exports, reporting dashboards), design
  against their own 15 rules rather than assuming they need hand-holding — they will notice if it's
  sloppy by their own standard.
